UNIVERSAL BOOK WRITING, REVIEW, AND PUBLISHING PRINCIPLES
Finland-based publication and English-language international editions
REUSABLE MASTER POLICY — applies to present and future books in any subject area
Maintainer: Arman Golbidi | Edition 2.0 | Research checkpoint: 21 October 2026
============================================================================================================
PURPOSE, SCOPE, AND HOW TO APPLY THIS MASTER DOCUMENT
- The present file is a reusable publishing framework, not a Snowflake-only checklist and not an individual book outline.
- Use the universal editorial standards for EVERY book; activate only relevant optional genre and product modules.
- For every new title, create a short Book-Specific Profile recording scope, audience, length, language variety, format, publisher, sources, and review plan.
- This master policy is written entirely in English. English manuscripts published from Finland do not become Finnish-language manuscripts merely because of the publisher location.
- Finnish legal and commercial issues are documented in their own section; rules marked LEGAL FACT require a fresh pre-publication check.
- Where law or contractual obligations vary with facts (retail channel, product type, nationality, publisher location, VAT status), confirm the applicability rather than assuming it.
- Labels: [LEGAL FACT] = grounded rule; [VERIFY CASE] = conditional compliance check; [QUALITY STANDARD] = the author’s editorial rule; [OPTIONAL] = only for relevant genres.
- This is editorial and research guidance, not individualized legal, tax, or immigration advice. Finnish/Swedish legislation controls over unofficial English translations.
- The Snowflake manuscript has its own companion: Snowflake_Book_Detailed_Outline_EN.txt. Other books receive different outlines but retain this policy.
- Check the authoritative sources at the end of this document again before signing a publishing contract, sending files to print, or offering a commercial product.

01 | BOOK IDENTITY, PROMISE, AND MARKET POSITIONING
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
01.01 Define the transformation promised to the reader in one defensible sentence; avoid empty superlatives.
01.02 Identify a primary reader persona, relevant secondary personas, buying motivations, and reader limitations.
01.03 State what will be learned, experienced, or understood, and what is explicitly outside scope.
01.04 Study comparable books, their audience reviews, table of contents, strengths, weaknesses, and publication dates.
01.05 Document genuine differentiation: explanation, evidence, narrative voice, originality, usability, or source access.
01.06 Define book category and genre consistently across proposal, jacket copy, metadata, and retail classification.
01.07 Decide whether the work is original nonfiction, academic reference, textbook, professional guide, fiction, biography, or a hybrid.
01.08 Draft a one-page positioning statement and test it with at least three target readers when possible.
01.09 Avoid unauthorized claims of endorsement, association, academic approval, or publisher affiliation.
01.10 Adopt a working title and subtitle; verify potential trademark confusion and already-published title collisions.

============================================================================================================
02 | READER RESEARCH AND INCLUSIVE AUDIENCE DESIGN
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
02.01 Specify assumed baseline knowledge, language proficiency, equipment, and accessibility needs.
02.02 Map difficult concepts and the vocabulary the reader needs before encountering them.
02.03 Write a reader journey from opening pages to the promised outcome; flag prerequisite gaps.
02.04 Include distinct paths only when they solve real differences in reader readiness.
02.05 Choose explanation and illustration styles that do not rely on a narrow cultural background.
02.06 Use clear language without concealing necessary technical precision or scientific uncertainty.
02.07 Avoid stereotypes and unsupported assumptions about geography, disability, income, or education.
02.08 Obtain reader feedback from people similar to the actual audience rather than only colleagues.
02.09 For advanced books, provide an entry roadmap and definitions instead of pretending prerequisite knowledge is universal.
02.10 Document scope decisions that protect beginners from overload and protect experts from superficiality.

03 | PROJECT-SPECIFIC BOOK PROFILE AND CHANGE CONTROL
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
03.01 Create a Book Profile for each new project with title, topic, genre, audience, purpose, and content boundaries.
03.02 Record author and publisher roles, place of publication, distribution channels, target languages, and formats.
03.03 Set realistic page or word targets; a strict 300-page target is a Snowflake-specific choice, not a universal rule.
03.04 Specify UK English or US English, spelling dictionary, citation system, heading capitalization, and typography.
03.05 List authoritative sources, reviewers, licensed datasets/images, and planned companion resources.
03.06 Set chapter owners, review deadlines, version IDs, issue-tracking procedure, and release checkpoints.
03.07 Maintain a decision log; changes to premise, genre, pagination, rights, or planned audience need an explicit record.
03.08 Preserve old drafts and a readable change history in version control or a reliable document-management system.
03.09 Separate approved manuscript claims from brainstorming, unverified suggestions, and future additions.
03.10 For later books, copy only this profile template and activate the relevant optional modules.

============================================================================================================
04 | OUTLINE, CONTENT ARCHITECTURE, AND PAGE BUDGET
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
04.01 Design the table of contents as a logical progression, not an unordered catalogue of ideas.
04.02 Assign to every chapter a purpose, outcome, key arguments or scenes, and place in the whole work.
04.03 Map subsection dependencies so a reader does not need unexplained knowledge from later chapters.
04.04 Provide a realistic budget for front matter, body, references, index, and back matter; verify its arithmetic.
04.05 Treat planned page ranges as estimates until final typesetting, unless the publisher specifies a firm production constraint.
04.06 Reserve room for citations, diagrams, tables, exercises, acknowledgements, and index entries.
04.07 Reduce redundancy through cross-references; do not force important topics into implausibly short sections.
04.08 Move appropriate full source code, raw datasets, and auxiliary exercises into a stable companion repository for technical texts.
04.09 For fiction or narrative nonfiction, budget by beats, acts, scenes, or narrative units rather than arbitrary miniature subsections.
04.10 Set a content-freeze date and a procedure for resolving late scope changes.

============================================================================================================
05 | EVIDENCE, CLAIM TAXONOMY, AND VERIFICATION
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
05.01 Classify claims as primary-source fact, interpreted finding, empirical observation, expert opinion, scenario assumption, or fictional element.
05.02 Build a claim ledger: claim ID, manuscript location, source, page/URL, access date, confidence, reviewer, and verification status.
05.03 Trace every numerical or historical fact to inspectable evidence; do not cite material that only appears to support it.
05.04 When a result is obtained by the author, disclose procedure, sample, context, and limitations.
05.05 Distinguish actual measurements from illustrative simulations, synthetic numbers, and hypothetical examples.
05.06 Avoid claims such as guaranteed, always, best, risk-free, and legally compliant without proof and qualifications.
05.07 For controversial claims, seek independent high-quality corroboration and present relevant uncertainty fairly.
05.08 Prefer original documents and methods papers to summaries when interpreting technical or policy assertions.
05.09 Check whether facts were current at writing time but have expired by publication time.
05.10 A fluent AI-generated statement is not itself evidence, and fabricated citations are an immediate release blocker.

============================================================================================================
06 | CITATION HIERARCHY AND BIBLIOGRAPHY
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
06.01 Prefer original legislation, regulator instructions, standard documents, official technical documentation, and primary research where relevant.
06.02 Use peer-reviewed literature and scholarly monographs for scientific methods, their evidence quality, and historical context.
06.03 Use journalism and expert analysis for attributed context, not as an unexamined substitute for primary evidence.
06.04 Treat forums, social posts, and blogs as dated community opinion unless their empirical evidence can be verified.
06.05 Choose one citation style for the full work (author–date, numbered, footnotes, or a publisher-required style).
06.06 Cite both quotations and paraphrased material; indicate page or section wherever this helps the reader locate the support.
06.07 Maintain full bibliographic metadata: author, title, organization, edition, publication/update date, DOI/ISBN/URL and access date where appropriate.
06.08 Confirm each DOI, URL, author spelling, title, and year; never infer missing bibliographic details.
06.09 Link figures, tables, and reproduced excerpts to a separate rights ledger as well as to their bibliographic sources.
06.10 Use a reference manager or reproducible bibliography file and run a broken-link and duplicate-reference audit.

============================================================================================================
07 | RESEARCH METHOD AND FAIRNESS
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
07.01 Write the research question and inclusion/exclusion criteria before gathering material where feasible.
07.02 Record search dates, databases, query terms, selection decisions, and known blind spots for research-heavy nonfiction.
07.03 Differentiate exploratory reading from systematic review; do not call a process systematic unless it meets an explicit protocol.
07.04 Represent competing interpretations proportionately to the supporting evidence without false equivalence.
07.05 Check denominator, base rate, uncertainty, sampling, and causal inference in all statistical statements.
07.06 Identify conflicts of interest, promotional sources, sponsored evidence, and methodological weaknesses.
07.07 Explain negative findings and evidence gaps rather than inventing a neat conclusion.
07.08 If interviews are used, record consent, accuracy-check procedures, source protection, and permissions.
07.09 For memoir and biography, separate personal recollection, documents, quotations, and reconstruction.
07.10 Record a dated research audit trail that an independent reviewer can inspect.

============================================================================================================
08 | CHAPTER AND SECTION WRITING BLUEPRINT
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
08.01 Begin a nonfiction chapter with its question, stakes, prerequisites if relevant, and intended learning outcome.
08.02 Establish the key concepts before presenting specialized methods or exceptions.
08.03 For each important choice, compare alternatives, assumptions, costs, and limitations.
08.04 Support abstract concepts with one precise example, worked calculation, diagram, scenario, or documented case.
08.05 Identify frequent misconceptions, edge cases, and what can go wrong in practice.
08.06 Clearly distinguish steps the reader should execute from illustrative or pseudocode-only passages.
08.07 End with a concise synthesis, reflection or practice questions, and a bridge to the next chapter.
08.08 Adapt the blueprint for fiction: dramatic question, scene goal, conflict, consequence, and emotional continuity.
08.09 Define expected artifacts for how-to books and interpretive outcomes for analytic or narrative books.
08.10 Keep cross-chapter terminology and promises consistent in the manuscript.

============================================================================================================
09 | ENGLISH-LANGUAGE STYLE GUIDE
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
09.01 Choose British or American English and consistently apply it to spelling, punctuation, dates, and quotations.
09.02 Use descriptive headings with one capitalization rule across the manuscript and online resources.
09.03 Prefer clear subject-verb sentences; preserve nuance where complexity genuinely requires it.
09.04 Expand acronyms at first use, and define specialized terminology in a controlled glossary.
09.05 Use consistent capitalization of proper names, command names, units, variables, and product names.
09.06 Check the placement and rendering of references, citations, footnotes, equations, and code symbols.
09.07 Avoid hype, unexplained jargon, filler, repetition, and misleading expressions such as simply click.
09.08 Use inclusive phrasing while retaining contextually necessary distinctions in scientific and narrative work.
09.09 Test edited prose with a subject specialist and an intended nonexpert reader where both are appropriate.
09.10 Employ a native-level or professional English copyeditor for a final consistency pass when feasible.

============================================================================================================
10 | SCIENTIFIC AND TECHNICAL ACCURACY
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.01 Verify definitions, equations, units, boundary conditions, algorithms, version numbers, and diagrams.
10.02 Write reproducible derivations and show assumptions rather than skipping critical reasoning steps.
10.03 Distinguish theoretical properties from empirical behavior under a measured workload.
10.04 For products and services, record platform/version, edition, region, feature status, and observation date.
10.05 Never describe beta or preview availability as universal or permanent.
10.06 State dataset size, data generation procedure, and hardware/software environment for numerical results.
10.07 Include failure cases, counterexamples, and limitations alongside strengths.
10.08 Do not present a vendor's public roadmap as a contractual guarantee of future capability.
10.09 Request an independent technical review of calculations, security-sensitive material, and code.
10.10 Mark details that should be reverified immediately before production or print approval.

============================================================================================================
11 | EXECUTABLE EXAMPLES AND COMPANION MATERIALS
CLASSIFICATION: OPTIONAL MODULE FOR TECHNICAL / HOW-TO BOOKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.01 Every runnable code sample must have a source file in the versioned repository or a documented downloadable archive.
11.02 Supply dependency versions, operating assumptions, input fixtures, expected outputs, and cleanup commands.
11.03 Mark pseudocode clearly so the reader never mistakes explanatory fragments for tested commands.
11.04 Keep private credentials, personal identifiers, and sensitive company data out of both text and version control history.
11.05 Use parameterized inputs, least privilege, and safe defaults for destructive commands or billable services.
11.06 Implement unit, integration, smoke, and regression checks appropriate to the software artifact.
11.07 Use small deterministic inputs for introductory examples and tolerances for stochastic results.
11.08 Record expensive prerequisites and offer a low-cost or offline alternative where possible.
11.09 Provide a complete setup -> run -> validate -> tear-down workflow that a new reviewer can follow.
11.10 Tag the repository to correspond exactly to the released edition rather than pointing only to a moving main branch.

============================================================================================================
12 | EXPERIMENTS, BENCHMARKS, CHARTS, AND CALCULATIONS
CLASSIFICATION: OPTIONAL MODULE FOR EMPIRICAL / TECHNICAL BOOKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.01 Define a specific hypothesis, baseline, outcome metric, and relevance to the reader.
12.02 Record sample size, measurement conditions, repetitions, parameters, and collection dates.
12.03 Preserve raw observations and transformation code when licensing, privacy, and size allow.
12.04 Show units, uncertainty ranges, axis scales, comparison baselines, and relevant confounders.
12.05 Do not mix cached and uncached timings, different hardware, or different workloads without disclosure.
12.06 Never improve performance by silently sacrificing correctness or using noncomparable outputs.
12.07 Publish failed or null results when they affect the book's recommendations.
12.08 Disclose selection effects, reproducibility constraints, and threats to validity.
12.09 Label all invented or toy datasets as synthetic or illustrative, including illustrative financial figures.
12.10 Recheck every chart against source values before press-ready PDF export.

============================================================================================================
13 | PRIVACY, CONFIDENTIAL SOURCES, AND DATA PROTECTION
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13.01 Inventory any personal data, interviews, account details, customer datasets, screenshots, or identifiable stories.
13.02 Minimize personal information and define a defensible lawful basis for handling personal data where the GDPR applies.
13.03 Avoid treating pseudonyms as irreversible anonymization; identify residual reidentification risks.
13.04 Seek consent or other appropriate rights where legally necessary, especially for photos and interviews.
13.05 Protect unpublished manuscripts, sources, and reader mailing-list data with access controls and backups.
13.06 Use synthetic examples when real personal data have no essential educational value.
13.07 Review confidentiality agreements and institutional permissions before reproducing employer or client material.
13.08 Include a privacy notice and appropriate processing arrangements for a direct-to-consumer newsletter or web store if applicable.
13.09 Check data storage, international transfers, retention, and deletion for personal-data workflows.
13.10 Refer special cases to qualified privacy counsel rather than claiming every historical or journalistic publication has the same GDPR handling.

============================================================================================================
14 | SENSITIVE, REGULATED, AND HIGH-STAKES CONTENT
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14.01 Identify whether the book gives medical, legal, financial, safety-critical, or similar high-impact advice.
14.02 Obtain topic-qualified independent professional review and verify authoritative standards at the correct jurisdiction and date.
14.03 Differentiate educational context from individualized professional services and diagnosis.
14.04 Do not imply a general disclaimer cures inaccurate, negligent, or unsafe substantive content.
14.05 Include contraindications, limitations, emergency pathways, and uncertainty when genuinely relevant.
14.06 Check that exercises, laboratory demonstrations, and procedural instructions do not expose readers to unreasonable danger.
14.07 Avoid publishing unsafe real credentials, exploitable production instructions, or dangerous physical handling steps as default examples.
14.08 Use extra scrutiny for advice involving children, protected populations, regulated products, or personal data.
14.09 Maintain a correction priority for safety-critical errors and a rapid public errata mechanism.
14.10 Validate the final manuscript against relevant professional practice and consumer-protection standards.

============================================================================================================
15 | ILLUSTRATIONS, TABLES, DIAGRAMS, AND MEDIA
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15.01 Each visual must answer a specific reader question or carry indispensable narrative meaning.
15.02 Number and title charts and tables consistently, with captions, sources, and explanatory footnotes.
15.03 Prefer editable vector source assets and keep print-resolution originals in the asset repository.
15.04 Show units, legend, time window, data provenance, and uncertainty for quantitative visuals.
15.05 Ensure diagrams remain meaningful in grayscale and do not encode critical differences solely in color.
15.06 Redact names, addresses, email addresses, account numbers, tokens, and secrets from screenshots.
15.07 Track image authorship, owner, license, territorial and format permissions, credit language, and expiration dates.
15.08 Obtain written rights for third-party covers, photos, paintings, diagrams, and substantial illustrations when required.
15.09 Use original diagrams for explanation, but still cite a distinct source if the underlying design or facts come from it.
15.10 Do not assume a Google Images result, official webpage, or social-media image is free to reproduce.

============================================================================================================
16 | ACCESSIBILITY AND INCLUSIVE FORMATTING
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16.01 Use correct semantic heading hierarchy, tagged structure when supported, and meaningful navigation landmarks.
16.02 Write effective alternative text for meaningful images; mark genuinely decorative visuals appropriately.
16.03 Give tables true header relationships and avoid making important data an inaccessible bitmap.
16.04 Test reflow, font enlargement, screen-reader navigation, text selection, and keyboard controls in relevant digital formats.
16.05 Use readable contrast, typographic hierarchy, distinct link text, and line spacing.
16.06 Provide text alternatives to essential information embedded in video or audio where relevant.
16.07 Use accessible EPUB production and validate against EPUB Accessibility 1.1 and applicable standards.
16.08 Test exported PDF navigation/bookmarks, selectable text, and reading order; do not assert PDF/UA compliance without testing.
16.09 Create accessibility metadata and a truthful accessibility statement where it is required.
16.10 For Finland/EU commercial e-book distribution, apply the jurisdiction-specific obligations in sections 39–43 below.

============================================================================================================
17 | ETHICAL AND ACCOUNTABLE USE OF AI TOOLS
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17.01 Treat AI outputs as provisional source material requiring human editorial and factual verification.
17.02 Keep identifiable authorial responsibility for accuracy, originality, rights clearance, and final approval.
17.03 Log significant AI roles in drafting, editing, research, translation, code generation, or illustration production.
17.04 Verify every AI-suggested citation against an actual document; reject invented DOIs, fabricated quotations, and false page numbers.
17.05 Avoid uploading confidential manuscripts, personal data, or contract-protected content to unapproved AI services.
17.06 Check a publisher or distributor's disclosure rules for AI-generated text, images, narration, or translations.
17.07 Distinguish machine-assisted editing from fully machine-generated content if the platform or contract requires this.
17.08 Keep provenance of generated visuals and respect the rights of people depicted and third-party materials.
17.09 Do not assign authorship or scholarly credibility to AI tools in place of responsible human contributors.
17.10 Adopt a transparent, accurate AI-use note when meaningful AI contributions would matter to readers or partners.

============================================================================================================
18 | COPYRIGHT, PERMISSIONS, AND TRADEMARK DILIGENCE
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18.01 Assume third-party creative expression is protected until an applicable license, statutory exception, or permission supports use.
18.02 Separate rights for text, code, datasets, graphics, maps, photographs, interviews, translations, and recorded audio.
18.03 Store written licenses and permissions showing scope, medium, edition, duration, language, territory, and credit terms.
18.04 Do not confuse attribution with permission; credit alone usually does not confer a right to reproduce copyrighted work.
18.05 Use quotation exceptions only when the statutory conditions and the purpose of quotation support them.
18.06 Verify the conditions of Creative Commons and other licenses, including commercial use and share-alike requirements.
18.07 Check compatibility of licensed components before combining them into a paid book or public repository.
18.08 Do not imply sponsorship from a product name, logo, institution, or vendor; use accurate independent-work disclaimers.
18.09 Review potential title/brand confusion and cover-image rights before distribution.
18.10 Maintain a rights ledger and remove unresolved third-party material before final print approval.

19 | PUBLISHING CONTRACTS AND AUTHORSHIP AGREEMENTS
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19.01 Identify the legal publisher, imprint, author, contributors, and any coauthor or ghostwriting arrangements.
19.02 Review grants of print, e-book, audio, translation, adaptation, territory, and sublicensing rights separately.
19.03 Inspect exclusivity, term, out-of-print/reversion triggers, and rights retained for future works or courses.
19.04 Define royalty calculation basis, discounting, returns, accounting periods, and payment timing.
19.05 Agree on responsibilities for editing, marketing, rights permissions, legal review, indexes, proofs, accessibility, and deposits.
19.06 Check warranties, indemnities, liability caps, and dispute-resolution clauses carefully before signing.
19.07 Specify who controls final edits, title/cover decisions, supplemental materials, and error corrections.
19.08 Provide rules for derivative works, updates, translations, new editions, and companion website ownership.
19.09 If contributors or editors own original illustrations or text, obtain appropriate publication rights in writing.
19.10 Seek a Finnish publishing lawyer or competent adviser for commercially consequential contract provisions.

20 | EDITORIAL REVIEW LAYERS
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
20.01 Developmental edit: verify the book's promise, scope, flow, omissions, repetition, and reader journey.
20.02 Subject-matter review: verify arguments, calculations, terminology, evidence and technical statements.
20.03 Copyedit: correct grammar, punctuation, spelling, style, citations, and internal consistency.
20.04 Sensitivity or domain-context review: use qualified readers when content involves represented communities or vulnerable persons.
20.05 Fact-check: match key claims and quotes to primary evidence with recorded corrections.
20.06 Proofread: check the locked layout, page breaks, typographic errors, captions, tables, and cross-references.
20.07 For a textbook, conduct learner usability testing of representative chapters and exercises.
20.08 For fiction, use beta readers to evaluate plot, character consistency, pacing, and structural logic.
20.09 Maintain a severity system: blocker, major, minor, and editorial preference; assign owners and due dates.
20.10 No release proceeds while unresolved accuracy, rights, safety, or privacy blockers remain.

============================================================================================================
21 | INDEPENDENT REVIEW AND REPRODUCIBILITY
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
21.01 A reviewer should be able to locate all evidence without needing the author to explain undocumented steps.
21.02 Include a reviewer packet: current manuscript, issue tracker, sources, test evidence, rights ledger, and open questions.
21.03 Confirm that representative examples, calculations, or directions work from a clean environment where practical.
21.04 Use a second reviewer for consequential claims and for areas outside the author's primary expertise.
21.05 Record disagreements and the resolution rationale; do not erase the existence of significant disputes.
21.06 Require reviewers to distinguish factual corrections from stylistic preferences.
21.07 Capture final reviewer sign-off with manuscript edition, review date, and exclusions.
21.08 Disclose in-book peer review only if actual independent peers reviewed the work in the claimed manner.
21.09 Clearly separate invited editorial feedback from formal academic peer review.
21.10 Credit contributors only with permission and with accurate role descriptions.

============================================================================================================
22 | EXERCISES AND LEARNING ASSESSMENT
CLASSIFICATION: OPTIONAL MODULE FOR TEXTBOOKS / GUIDES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
22.01 Each exercise must test a previously stated learning objective rather than introduce an unexplained feature.
22.02 Give the starting state, necessary equipment or data, time/effort expectation, and success criteria.
22.03 Provide at least one worked example for especially difficult procedures or calculations.
22.04 Write tasks at foundation, applied, and advanced levels when the audience benefits from tiering.
22.05 Publish a reference answer, rubric, or evaluation checklist where appropriate.
22.06 Reward sound reasoning and correct constraints, not just one arbitrary implementation.
22.07 Mark optional exercises that require paid services, sensitive data, or specialized hardware.
22.08 Test that a learner can follow the instructions without access to the author's private setup.
22.09 For noninstructional titles, replace exercises with discussion prompts, reading guides, or no exercises at all.
22.10 Check exercise copyright and dataset sharing permissions independently of the manuscript.

23 | DATA, SOURCE ARCHIVES, AND COMPANION REPOSITORIES
CLASSIFICATION: OPTIONAL MODULE FOR DATA / TECHNICAL / RESEARCH BOOKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
23.01 List every external data source with creator, acquisition date, license, geographic coverage, units, and update frequency.
23.02 Preserve snapshots or hashes when the applicable license allows archival redistribution.
23.03 Store raw data separately from transformed data and document every significant preprocessing step.
23.04 Provide a readme describing setup, provenance, quality checks, expected outputs, and known missing values.
23.05 Publish only datasets for which redistribution rights have been verified; otherwise give lawful access instructions.
23.06 Publish code under a deliberate license and the book's prose under its own publication terms.
23.07 Do not assume a code license covers its embedded images, third-party dependencies, or datasets.
23.08 Add issue templates for bugs, errata, security issues, and questions about exercises or datasets.
23.09 Version companion resources together with each book edition and preserve accessible release archives.
23.10 Include a maintenance end date or support policy instead of promising perpetual availability.

24 | LAYOUT, TYPOGRAPHY, AND PRINT PRODUCTION
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
24.01 Obtain trim size, binding, bleed, safe-area, spine, color-profile, embedded-font, and paper specifications from the printer.
24.02 Create page templates and a hierarchy for part titles, chapter openers, running headers, footers, and body type.
24.03 Choose fonts and licenses suitable for both printed and electronic distribution.
24.04 Check orphan/widow lines, hyphenation, paragraph breaks, long URLs, table splits, equations, and wide code blocks.
24.05 Ensure source files and export PDFs include embedded fonts and adequate image resolution as required by the printer.
24.06 Use a print-ready PDF with correct crop/bleed settings; validate it with the specific print service.
24.07 Test grayscale legibility for charts and graphics if economical black-and-white editions are planned.
24.08 Inspect sample spreads and the final physical proof for binding, margins, color shifts, page order, and cut-off text.
24.09 Keep reader-facing page numbers synchronized with printed contents, cross-references, tables, and indexes.
24.10 Save master source, print PDF, accessible digital outputs, and deterministic build notes as distinct artifacts.

25 | E-BOOK AND DIGITAL PRODUCTION
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
25.01 Specify which products are sold: PDF, reflowable EPUB, fixed-layout EPUB, audiobook, or subscription access.
25.02 Define format-specific acceptance criteria, supported reading applications, and accessibility metadata.
25.03 Use real text, heading semantics, table markup, and navigation; avoid rasterizing the entire e-book.
25.04 Test EPUB on at least two reading engines and an assistive-technology workflow where feasible.
25.05 Use valid metadata: title, author, language, identifiers, publication date, rights, and accessibility properties.

25.07 Ensure DRM decisions do not block legally required assistive features in applicable markets.
25.08 Do not treat a visually attractive PDF as proof that an e-book meets accessibility obligations.
25.09 Maintain separate build outputs, ISBNs when applicable, and revision records for each product format.
25.10 Test purchase-to-download and offline-use behavior before public release.

26 | FRONT MATTER AND BACK MATTER COMPLETENESS
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
26.01 Front matter: half title/title page, author/publisher identification, copyright page, ISBN information where used.
26.02 Preface or introduction: why the book exists, audience, assumptions, contributions and known limitations.
26.03 Table of contents: consistent chapter and section names, accurate links or page numbers.
26.04 Credits: image attribution, contributor roles, licensed material acknowledgements, and funding statements.
26.05 For technical books: prerequisites, version notes, setup warnings, and reproducibility instructions.
26.06 Back matter: bibliography, glossary, appendix material, and genuinely useful index where appropriate.
26.07 Provide a stable correction page or errata URL and edition/printing date.
26.08 Include any legally or contractually required product notices, warnings, or publisher metadata.
26.09 Do not invent an ISBN, DOI, endorsement, publisher, reviewer, or affiliation before it exists.
26.10 Proof all preliminary pages against the publisher's requirements and the final release manifest.

27 | METADATA, DISCOVERABILITY, AND TRUTHFUL MARKETING
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
27.01 Maintain canonical title, subtitle, authors, edition, publication date, keywords, categories, language, and formats.
27.02 Write a specific and truthful description that reflects the actual manuscript, not intended chapters never completed.
27.03 Use accurate author biography and professional credentials without exaggeration.
27.04 Verify retailer keywords, search categories, format labels, and sample previews against publication terms.
27.05 Credit contributors, cover illustrators, designers, and translators according to contracts and custom.
27.06 Avoid misleading comparisons or unverifiable claims about rankings, endorsements, sales, or official status.
27.07 Confirm trademark use in metadata and cover art, especially for product-based technical books.
27.08 State compatibility/version cutoffs where a reader may depend on time-sensitive instructions.
27.09 Prepare an accessible public description and a cover image that remains legible in thumbnail size.
27.10 Maintain metadata parity across the ISBN agency, distributor feeds, website, and actual imprint page.

28 | BUDGETING, OPERATIONS, AND COMMERCIAL RISK
CLASSIFICATION: PROJECT QUALITY STANDARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.01 Prepare a per-format budget for editing, illustrations, professional review, rights clearance, indexing, design, and proofs.
28.02 Include ISBN administration workflow, distribution fees, printing, legal deposits, storage, shipping, and returns.
28.03 Calculate break-even at plausible price and sales scenarios, including taxes, payment fees, and retailer margins.
28.04 Identify who is responsible for invoicing, VAT, accounting, fulfillment, refunds, and buyer support.
28.05 Document risks of currency conversion, cross-border shipping, and e-book platform policies.
28.06 Do not promise readers a fixed third-party product price if it changes over time.

39 | MANDATORY LEGAL DEPOSIT OF PRINTED BOOKS
CLASSIFICATION: FINLAND — LEGAL DEPOSIT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
39.01 [LEGAL FACT] The Act on Collecting and Preserving Cultural Materials (1433/2007) requires legal deposit of publications distributed in Finland, subject to type-specific rules. [FI04]
39.02 [LEGAL FACT] The National Library says printed books normally require SIX deposit copies; the rule also covers print-on-demand and noncommercial distribution. [FI04] [FI05]
39.03 [LEGAL FACT] Where a book is printed in Finland, the printing house generally submits the required copies; when printed abroad or self-printed, the publisher generally submits them. [FI05]
39.04 [LEGAL FACT] For very small print runs of fewer than 50 copies, the publisher may APPLY for a reduced deposit obligation; a smaller count is not an automatic exemption. [FI05]
39.05 [LEGAL FACT] Changed or updated editions and distinct print formats can require fresh legal-deposit action; unchanged reprints under the same identifier may not. [FI04] [FI05]
39.06 [QUALITY STANDARD] Confirm the current packing list, timing, submission instructions, and deposit office details before distribution.
